An Agile mindset with a data-first approach

Two habits shape every engagement. They are why the automations get used, and why their value can be measured.

Agile by default

I ship the highest-value workflow first, then improve it sprint by sprint. Every automation lives in a backlog with a Definition of Ready and a Definition of Done, is demoed at the end of the sprint, and gets better from what the retro surfaces.

Data before AI

Before any model touches a process, I inventory the data, write the data dictionary, and baseline the KPIs. Diagnostic analysis finds the real bottleneck, and the same KPIs prove the automation worked.

SalesSDRPresales

A revenue engine, automated from lead to outreach

The situation
Lead research, account signals and outreach were spread across a CRM, LinkedIn, data providers and email.
What I built
Automation suites for sales, sales development and presales: morning lead digests per rep, account discovery, lead generation with memory and CRM sync, tiered account signals, and bench-to-job matching.
What changed
Reps start the day with researched, ranked leads in their inbox, saving an estimated 30 to 45 minutes each morning. Known companies never resurface, and outreach goes out only after a person approves it.

Supply chainMulti-agent

Tender evaluation intelligence

The situation
Evaluating bids means checking technical compliance, normalising commercial offers and weighing risk across many suppliers.
What I did
Product ownership and solution design for four autonomous agents: technical compliance, deviation classification, commercial normalisation and risk-weighted ranking, under a governance and audit layer.
What changed
A defined MVP, agent specifications with cross-agent dependencies, and a synthetic demo tender that tests each agent against real evaluation stages.
